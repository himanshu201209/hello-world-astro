Have you ever truly loved your job?

Not simply appreciated or tolerated it, but felt a genuine sense of joy in the work you were doing and the team you were doing it with.

I have been thinking about that question since micro1 began tracking its Expert Happiness Index.

When the latest results were published, I was proud to see one of the data pipelines I led ranked among the top, with some of the happiest experts across micro1.

The responses were encouraging across the board. But this result felt especially meaningful because it reflected something I had already sensed through working closely with the team.

Experts have told me that micro1 is the best place they have ever worked. Others have chosen to continue working with us even when presented with opportunities elsewhere.

They stayed because of the people, the community, and the feeling that their contributions were genuinely valued.

Expert happiness is far more than a feedback metric. It is core to what we are building at micro1.

We are in the talent business.

As AI systems become more advanced, reinforcement learning (RL) and model evaluation increasingly require sophisticated, specialized knowledge. Experts have options.

Yet too often, human data work is designed as though the humans themselves are interchangeable.

Teams over-recruit. Onboarding is rushed. Communication is poor. Contractors face aggressive deadlines with little context and may be removed from projects without meaningful explanation.

And all of this happens to professionals contributing years of hard-earned knowledge to some of the most consequential technology being built today.

A physician, lawyer, engineer, designer, or researcher can apply their expertise to technology that may affect how millions of people live and work.

And we are only at the beginning.

We are creating what is likely to become one of the largest job sectors the world has ever seen. We have a responsibility to build its foundation thoughtfully.

As this sector grows, the experience of contributing must feel as meaningful as the contribution itself.

When thinking about expert happiness, I am reminded of one of the core principles from my training at Harvard Medical School.

As a member of Peabody Society, I was introduced to one of Dr. Francis Peabody’s most enduring teachings: “the secret of the care of the patient is in caring for the patient.”

The same principle applies here.

The secret to expert happiness is caring about the experts.

Listening to them. Communicating honestly. Respecting their time. Recognizing their contributions. Building a community rather than merely assembling a workforce.

At micro1, we believe happier experts produce better work.

But even before considering the quality of the data, treating people with dignity is simply the right thing to do.

The most advanced technology of our time still depends on human judgment.

We should never lose sight of the humans behind it.
